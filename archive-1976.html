Where It All Started

The first meeting, the first road run and the people and motorcycles that formed the Tauranga Classic Motorcycle Club.

March 1976
Newsletter No. 1

Thirty people, one meeting — and a new club

Fifty years later, TCMCC is an established part of the local motorcycling scene. In March 1976 it was simply a new idea being tested by a group of enthusiasts — and the response surprised even the organisers.

“Well our first meeting got underway shortly after 8.00 p.m. with thirty people in attendance, far more than we had anticipated.”

Ross Simons was elected President, Jan Simons Secretary and Yvonne Allen Treasurer. Ivan Allen, C. Bell, L. Black and B. Brewer formed the first committee. Jack Ollerenshaw offered the Ascot Lounge in 11th Avenue for monthly meetings, and subscriptions were set at $5 a year — largely to cover the cost of producing the newsletters.

They were soon out riding

On Sunday 7 March, ten motorcycles left Tauranga at 10 a.m. for Maketu, with wives and children travelling in cars. Seven Rotorua motorcycles joined them. The bikes were lined up for the public to view, everyone enjoyed a picnic lunch, and the new club was already doing what it was created to do: ride, talk motorcycles and enjoy good company.

The motorcycles

The first newsletter's membership pages are a remarkable snapshot: a 1924 7-9 Harley-Davidson, a 1928 Indian Four, Coventry Eagle, BSA, Norton, Ariel, AJS, Matchless, Velocette and Royal Enfield machinery, alongside newer Triumphs, Ducatis, Suzukis and Kawasakis.

One entry says almost everything about the spirit of the new club. Beside Philip Tymon's name was simply: “Hope to purchase a bike.”

Newsletter No. 9

The club takes shape

By Newsletter No. 9, the fledgling organisation was clearly becoming a functioning motorcycle club. A run to Sapphire Springs attracted eleven machines. After lunch the riders headed to Karangahake Gorge to meet a BMW group, where roughly 40 motorcycles had gathered, some with sidecars.

Members also travelled to Matamata at the invitation of the Vincent Owners Club. The newsletter records an impressive collection of Vincents — some beautifully restored, others “not quite so good”, although all apparently performed well.

Ross won the raffle — a bottle of wine, described as “an early Christmas present for our babysitter.”

It is these little observations that make the old newsletters so valuable. They record not only the motorcycles and events, but the everyday character of club life.

Members Register • August 1978

From a meeting of 30 to a growing network

Only about two and a half years after the first meeting, the members register had grown to seven pages. The club's reach extended beyond Tauranga to Rotorua, Whakatāne, Te Puke, Paeroa, Auckland, Wanganui, New Plymouth, Kerikeri, Whangārei and elsewhere.

The register reads today almost like a catalogue of classic motorcycling. More importantly, it preserves the names of the people who built the early club and the machines that brought them together.

Fifty years later

Thirty people met one evening. Officers were elected. Someone offered a room for meetings. They decided five dollars was a reasonable subscription. Then they got their motorcycles out and went for a ride.

The motorcycles mattered, but from the first newsletter something else was already there: people helping one another, sharing knowledge and parts, travelling together and enjoying the company of others with the same interest.

And fifty years later, we're still riding.

Tauranga Classic Motorcycle Club • Established 1976 • Tauranga, New Zealand
